# Ahya_baselinetranscriptomes

This repository contains all datasets and Rcode necessary to reproduce figures and statistics from Walker et al. (in review). 
Title: "Cryptic lineage and transcriptomic baseline jointly predict heat resistance in Acropora hyacinthus"
Authors: Nia Walker, Erik Hanson, Clarence Lin, Steve Palumbi
Corresponding Author: Nia Walker niawalker13@gmail.com

Abstract: Coral heat stress resistance is critical to reef persistence as ocean warming intensifies, yet the transcriptomic 
mechanisms underlying variation in resistance remain poorly resolved. We evaluated heat stress resistance in 39 Acropora hyacinthus colonies 
from a single reef region in Palau's southern lagoon, defining resistance by the number of days to reach moderate bleaching under experimental 
heat stress. We identified two putative cryptic lineages among colonies; one lineage (Csp5) showed substantially higher survival than the 
other (84% vs. 14%), despite both lineages co-occurring in the same reef region. Baseline (pre-heat stress) transcriptomes differed strongly 
between low- and high-resistance corals, with ~1,500 genes differentially expressed (5.6% of the transcriptome) and the majority downregulated 
in low-resistance individuals. Co-expression analysis of these baseline differences identified three gene modules whose expression tracked 
resistance: modules elevated in resistant corals were associated with DNA repair, calcium signaling, and symbiosis-related processes, while a module 
elevated in susceptible corals was dominated by translation and ribosome biogenesis genes. These baseline differences largely disappeared 16 hours 
post-heat stress, when corals across resistance categories converged on a similar transcriptomic response. This pattern is consistent with frontloading, 
in which resistant corals may maintain constitutively elevated expression of stress-relevant genes rather than inducing them in response to stress. 
These results suggest that pre-stress transcriptomic state and cryptic lineage identity are both important predictors of coral thermal performance and 
provide implications for identifying resistance biomarkers and guiding reef restoration efforts.
